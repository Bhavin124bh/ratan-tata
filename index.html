About

Ratan Naval Tata (28 December 1937 – 9 October 2024) was one of India's most respected industrialists and philanthropists. Born in Mumbai, he studied architecture and structural engineering at Cornell University before returning home to join the Tata Group in 1962, starting on the shop floor of Tata Steel in Jamshedpur.

He became Chairman of Tata Sons in 1991 and led the group for more than two decades, transforming a largely India-focused conglomerate into a global business with operations in over 100 countries. Under his leadership the group launched the Tata Indica, acquired Tetley, Corus and Jaguar Land Rover, and made Tata Consultancy Services a world-class technology company. He also championed affordable engineering through the Tata Nano.

Beyond business, he directed most of the group's profits to education, healthcare and rural development through the Tata Trusts, and he backed dozens of Indian startups as an angel investor. Known for humility, ethics and a love of dogs, aviation and design, he remains an inspiration to engineers and entrepreneurs.

Major Contributions

  1. Tata Indica (1998)

    India's first fully indigenously designed and manufactured passenger car, launched under his vision at Tata Motors.

  2. Tata Nano (2008)

    An ultra-low-cost people's car engineered to make safe four-wheel transport affordable for Indian families.

  3. Global Acquisitions

    Led the acquisitions of Tetley (2000), Corus Steel (2007) and Jaguar Land Rover (2008), taking the group global.

  4. Growth of TCS

    Backed Tata Consultancy Services as it went public in 2004 and became one of the world's largest IT services companies.

  5. Startup Investing & Innovation

    Mentored and invested in numerous Indian technology startups, encouraging a new generation of engineers and founders.

  6. Philanthropy via Tata Trusts

    Steered the Tata Trusts towards education, healthcare, clean water and rural development across India.
